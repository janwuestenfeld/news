News Pulse reads the headlines of 115 English news sources on Bluesky, Telegram and their own websites, minute by minute, across ten topics. For each topic and for the day as a whole it shows:

  • News Heat: how widely the news is covered
  • Breadth: how many sources report more serious news than they usually do
  • News Sentiment: whether it is positive or negative
  • News Velocity: how fast it arrives

Each reading compares today, up to the current time, with the same hours on comparable days. The Confluence tracker shows the three readings of each day side by side and marks the days when coverage and seriousness are both high (widely covered and serious), negative or positive when the news also leans clearly one way.

How to read the numbers: every 0–100 number compares today with comparable days (the same kind of day in the past 26 weeks; for News Sentiment, in the past year). 80 means higher than on 80 of 100 of them; 50 is a typical day.

What counts as negative or positiveNegative: escalation, new tariffs, a central bank raising rates or signalling it may, scarcer supply, bad company news. Positive: de-escalation and agreements, tariff cuts or deals, rate cuts, more plentiful supply, good company news. Crypto, Tech and AI, Fiscal: negative are hacks, bans, outages, layoffs, a shutdown or a downgrade; positive are approvals, launches, deals, investment or a budget deal. US politics (conflict or resolution) and Regulation (tightening or loosening) are not part of this scale.
