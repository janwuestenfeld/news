1. What News Pulse measures

News Pulse reads the headlines of 115 English-language news sources every 5 minutes. Each headline gets a topic, a grade for what it reports and a direction.

For ten topics and for the day as a whole, the page compares today, up to the current time, with the same hours on comparable days. Every 0 to 100 reading is a rank: 80 means today is higher than on 80 of 100 comparable days, and 50 is a typical day.

2. Sources and collection

Platforms

News Pulse reads 115 sources: 101 Bluesky accounts, 10 Telegram channels and 4 websites (Trading Economics, investingLive, Interfax and BNO News).

12 of them enter the readings on weekdays only, because their weekend history is still too short for a comparison.

How the headlines are read

  • Telegram: the public web preview of each channel, read forward from the last post already held.
  • Bluesky: the public author feed of each account, without replies. Posts seen in the previous 48 hours are remembered, so none is stored twice.
  • Websites: the Trading Economics news stream, Interfax's numbered pages, the investingLive weekly sitemap and the BNO News sitemap. New article pages are read for their title and time.
  • A collection run starts every 5 minutes on GitHub. All addresses are public and need no key. Every request names the collector as news-pulse-collector.
  • The posts of each run are stored as they arrive. After New York midnight they are merged into one file per day, with repeats dropped.

Entry tests

A source enters the readings when it passes seven structural tests; one that is short only on weekend history enters on weekdays only until its weekend history is long enough. No test of agreement with other sources and no reliability score decides entry.

  • English: at least 80% of its headlines read as English. 35 sources failed this test and are not collected.
  • Readable: the topic rules place at least 10% of its headlines in a topic.
  • Sound dates: on Bluesky, at least 99% of posts carry a creation time within one day of the time the network recorded them.
  • Own history: at least 60 comparable weekdays and 27 comparable weekend or holiday days, about 90 days of regular posting.
  • Alive: at least one post in the last 7 days, through a path that can be read live.
  • Own content: it posts at least half of its headlines first. A verbatim copy within 60 minutes counts for the first poster only. A source below half is a second route to the outlet it copies.
  • Kind: it is a news outlet and not an official issuer such as a central bank.

Every source is compared with its own past. A source that joins changes no other source's reading.

Repeats and reposts

Four rules keep one piece of news from counting twice within a source.

  • Adverts, channel notices, digests, session wraps and feature pieces are dropped before labelling.
  • A post that repeats the same source's earlier post word for word within 60 minutes is a repeat.
  • A story that has the same text as a story of the same source and topic in the previous 6 hours, or shares 70% of its words of four or more letters, is a repeat.
  • A story that shares 40% of its words with a story of the same source and topic in the previous 72 hours is a follow-up. Follow-ups and repeats stay out of every reading.

The counts strip

3. Labelling

Each headline passes four steps: topic rules, the grade, story grouping and the label model.

1. Topic rules

Keyword rules split each post into headline lines, give each line a first topic and an event type, and find the speaker. Lines that report a price level or move, or a schedule, leave the readings at this step.

2. Grade

Each line gets a grade from what it reports: the event's score, times a size factor, times a certainty factor. An attack or a strike grades higher than a threat, a statement or a forecast. The levels are in the codebook.

Most grades lie between 0.1 and 1. A stated size above the usual one can raise a grade to at most 2; 3% of notable stories are graded above 1.

A story is notable when its grade is 0.5 or more. The readings use notable stories only.

3. Stories

Within one source, lines on the same topic form one story when each follows the story's previous line within 30 minutes and names the same speaker or shares 30% of its words. A story's time is its first line; its grade is its highest line grade.

Across sources, stories on the same topic are grouped for the cards. A story joins an open group when the group's first story is at most 6 hours older and the two share distinctive words. Words are weighted by how rare they were in the topic's notable stories of the previous 28 days. The weighted overlap must reach 0.35, and at least two shared words must appear in a quarter of the group's stories. Groups never merge.

4. Label model

Every notable story goes to the label model, which gives its topic, its form and its direction. Stories below 0.5 keep the rules topic and carry no direction.

The model is an English sentence encoder (BGE-base, 109 million weights) fine-tuned with three output heads: topic, form and direction. It gives the topic and form of every story and the direction of Geopolitics, Trade, Central banks, Energy and commodities and Company news. The direction of Crypto, Tech and AI, Fiscal, US politics and Regulation comes from a second fine-tuned copy of the encoder (labeller version 6). All of them run on the CPU of the GitHub runner. No language model is called while the page runs.

The encoder was trained on 48,000 headlines labelled by a larger language model that followed the written instructions summarised in the codebook. Labeller version 5 was trained further on 12,832 headlines of the five newer topics labelled the same way under definitions fixed before any labelling. Labeller version 6 was trained further on the five topics' direction with 6,000 new US-politics headlines labelled the same way.

On 42 days kept out of training (4,705 headlines), the model gives the teacher's topic for 86.6% of headlines, its form for 94.9% and its direction for 73.5%. For Crypto, Tech and AI and Fiscal, labeller version 5 gave the teacher's direction for 77.2% of 1,217 headlines from those days. Labeller version 6, the version that runs, was checked on fresh headlines from those days read by an independent judge: for US politics it gives that direction for 71.5% of 919 (the opposite direction for 2.9%), and 15 of 25 lines it shows as resolution are right; for Regulation it gives the direction for 77.1% of 402 (opposite 8.0%), and 41 of 50 checked lines shown under Regulation are regulation.

The author judged 59 headlines blind; none was used for training or for writing the instructions. The model gives his direction for 48 of them and the opposite direction for 2. On his blind judgements of US politics and Regulation headlines, the directions shown agree with him on 15 of 19 and 15 of 20.

Form

A news report states a new event, statement, decision, forecast or data point, also when relayed from another outlet. Everything else is not a news report: opinion, columns, analysis pieces, explainers, newsletter or programme headings, lists, reviews, adverts, quizzes, history and anniversary pieces. A notable story that is not a news report leaves the readings.

Topic fixes after the model

Five fixed rules correct known borders after the model has labelled a story. They are listed with each topic in the codebook.

4. Codebook

The ten topics on the page, what each covers, what its two directions mean and where its borders lie. A story with no direction (0) counts in News Heat and Breadth, stays out of News Sentiment and counts for neither side. A line about something other than the topic it is shown under has no direction.

Geopolitics

Covers
War, military action, strikes, attacks, terrorism, threats of force, troop moves, drills, sanctions as security policy, ceasefires, hostages and prisoner exchanges, and diplomacy between states about security or war. Politics of other countries when it concerns security, war, sanctions or relations between states.
Negative (escalation)
Threats, buildups, attacks, killings, war starting or widening, new security sanctions, talks breaking down.
Positive (calming)
Ceasefires, truces, talks, agreements, withdrawals, releases of hostages or prisoners, easing of a blockade or of restrictions, de-escalating statements.
Borders
  • Attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz are Geopolitics.
  • A country's name does not set the topic: a line naming Iran is Geopolitics only when it reports a military, security or diplomatic event or threat; Iranian oil exports are Energy.
  • A labour strike, a trade war, a price war, a company that strikes a deal, a drone delivering food and a rocket launched into space are not military.
  • "Will not attack" is calming; "will not sign a peace deal" is escalation.

Trade

Covers
Tariffs, retaliation, trade talks and deals, export controls and trade restrictions, trade investigations, court rulings on tariffs.
Negative (escalation)
New or higher tariffs, tariff threats, retaliation, new export controls or sanctions, sanctions-evasion networks exposed, talks suspended, deals in doubt or opposed, costs of tariffs hitting firms or consumers.
Positive (de-escalation)
Tariff cuts, pauses, exemptions, extensions, agreements, talks progressing, a country or firm seeking an exemption, bills or court cases that would curb tariff powers, criticism of tariffs by officials, trade flows recovering.
Borders
  • Chip export controls are Trade.
  • On days when at least 3 sources post a notable line naming the Supreme Court together with tariffs, lines about that ruling move to Trade from Regulation, US politics, Company news, Central banks or Fiscal.
  • A line about a trade balance with no budget, tax, debt or government-deficit word moves from Fiscal to Trade.
  • "Will not impose tariffs" is de-escalation.

Central banks

Covers
Rate decisions, speeches and minutes of the Fed and other central banks; rate expectations attributed to officials, banks or analysts; central-bank personnel and independence; currency intervention.
Negative (hawkish)
Hikes; fewer, later or no cuts; cuts pushed back or unlikely; holding with a cut ruled out; balance-sheet reduction.
Positive (dovish)
Cuts, more or earlier cuts, the end of balance-sheet reduction.
Borders
  • The US President on Fed rates is Central banks.
  • "Cuts pushed to September" is hawkish. A negated hike has no direction; a negated cut is hawkish.

Energy

Covers
Oil, gas, OPEC, energy shipping, metals and agricultural supply news, commodity exports and imports, commodity price moves.
Negative (supply tighter)
Outages, output cuts, disruptions, attacks on energy infrastructure, a producing sector in decline, demand rising, prices up.
Positive (supply looser)
Supply increases, new projects and pipelines, weak demand, reserve releases, restarts, prices down.

Company news

Covers
Earnings, guidance, mergers and acquisitions, listings, management, company legal actions, analyst notes on a single company, company debt and share sales, a single stock's move.
Negative (bad for the company)
Losses, warnings, bankruptcies, calls for a chief to resign, new costs imposed, probes.
Positive (good for the company)
Higher spending plans, stake increases, listing plans and filings, wins, upgrades.
Borders
  • A court ruling against a firm in a private lawsuit is Company news.

US politics

Covers
Elections, polls, appointments, Congress, immigration, health and social policy, scandals in the United States.
Conflict
Standoffs and blocked votes, shutdown fights, lawsuits against or by the administration, states or Congress, courts blocking or overturning government action, indictments, investigations, subpoenas and impeachment moves against officials, firings and forced resignations, threats and attacks between politicians, protests and clashes, troop or National Guard deployments to cities, political violence, contested elections or results.
Resolution
Deals and compromises, bills passed with votes from both sides, a standoff or shutdown ending, lawsuits settled or dropped, charges or probes dropped, someone backing down or withdrawing a threat, a concession after an election, a court ruling that ends a dispute.
Borders
  • Conflict and resolution describe the state of a confrontation. The same event is coded the same way whoever is involved; the page shows both sides in neutral colours.
  • Polls, campaign events, plain election results, routine appointments and policy announcements with no opponent named have no direction.
  • A line about an election that names a foreign country and no American-only name or institution has no topic.
  • A line about the politics of another country leaves US politics and has no topic when a model trained on labelled lines gives it a probability of 0.7 or more of being foreign politics.
  • A court or legal line under Regulation with no regulator, market or economic-policy subject moves to US politics when it concerns the United States; otherwise it has no topic.
Measured accuracy
Direction agrees with an independent check on about 72% of fresh headlines; 60% of the lines shown as resolution are right.

Fiscal

Covers
Taxes, spending, budgets, deficits, the debt ceiling, shutdowns, entitlement programmes, government stakes in companies.
Negative
Shutdown starting, continuing or looming; funding bill blocked or failing; debt-limit standoff or default risk; deficit or debt larger than expected or at a record; revenue shortfall; rating downgrade or worse outlook; weak debt auction; breach of fiscal rules; a government falling over its budget; cost overruns or unfunded new obligations.
Positive
Budget or spending bill agreed or passed; shutdown averted or ended; debt limit raised or suspended; deficit or debt smaller than expected or narrowing; tax revenue above forecast or at a record; rating upgrade or better outlook; strong demand at a government debt auction; a consolidation plan agreed.
Borders
  • The direction describes the state of public finances and the budget process. A tax or spending measure reported only by who pays or receives has no direction.
  • A bill that cuts taxes and is passed is positive (a budget step completed); an official estimate that it widens the deficit is negative.
  • A line about a current or capital account with no budget, tax, debt or government-deficit word moves to Economic data.

Tech and AI

Covers
The AI sector and AI policy, chips, data centres, AI companies as a theme.
Negative
Outages and failures (failed launches, product recalls); bans, restrictions and export curbs on a firm or product; layoffs; lawsuits, probes and fines; security breaches of the firm; warnings of weak demand, losses, cost problems or an AI bubble; deals cancelled.
Positive
Launches and releases that work; approvals and licences; technical breakthroughs; deals, partnerships and acquisitions agreed; funding rounds and investment or data-centre plans; strong demand, sales or forecasts; chip supply or capacity expanding.
Borders
  • Policy debate with no decision, opinion and product news with no event have no direction.

Crypto

Covers
Crypto prices and flows, exchange-traded funds, exchanges, stablecoins, crypto regulation, crypto companies.
Negative
Hacks, exploits and thefts; bans and restrictions; enforcement actions, lawsuits, arrests and fraud; exchange outages, suspended withdrawals, insolvencies, delistings; outflows; liquidations, crashes and prices falling.
Positive
Fund approvals, listings and inflows; adoption by firms, banks or governments; laws or rulings that let crypto business proceed; partnerships, launches and funding rounds of crypto firms; prices or market value rising.
Borders
  • Token movements with no stated reason or effect, and hearings or votes only scheduled, have no direction.

Regulation

Covers
Financial and market regulation, regulators (SEC, CFTC, FDIC, FTC, FDA), antitrust, court rulings on economic policy.
Tightening
New rules, bans and requirements; probes, lawsuits, charges and fines by regulators or prosecutors against firms; antitrust suits and blocked mergers; licence reviews or revocations; higher capital or disclosure requirements; court rulings that uphold or extend rules; settlements that impose a penalty.
Loosening
Rules repealed, delayed, eased or exempted; approvals, licences and clearances granted, including merger approvals; probes, charges and cases dropped; lower capital or reporting requirements; court rulings that strike down or narrow rules.
Borders
  • Tightening and loosening describe the rules. The page shows both sides in neutral colours.
  • Hacks, cyber attacks, crimes and lawsuits between private parties with no regulator or rule involved have no direction under Regulation.
  • Lines about the death penalty have no topic.
Measured accuracy
82% of the lines shown under Regulation are regulation (41 of 50 checked); direction agrees with an independent check on 77% of fresh headlines.

Outside the ten topics

Economic data
Scheduled statistical releases of any country: labour, inflation, activity, surveys, inventories. They feed the Data releases card and no reading.
No topic
Market reports of prices, yields, currency levels and flows; sport, crime, accidents, weather and natural disasters without an attack; celebrities and human-interest stories; domestic politics of other countries with no security, trade or economic-policy content; adverts, channel notices, bare links and fragments.

5. Readings

News Heat

Per topic. The number of sources that carried at least one notable story on the topic today before the current time. A source counts once, however many stories it posts. Sources count when they posted any story today and have at least 8 valid comparable days.

For each comparable day, the same count is taken for the same sources at the same time. A source whose comparable day was thin (fewer than a quarter of its median daily stories) counts at its own carrying rate on its other comparable days. The reading is the share of comparable days with a lower count, ties counted half. Usual is the median count.

The gauge. For each topic, today's count minus its usual count. The largest of these is ranked against the largest on each comparable day at the same time. That topic is the day's top topic. At least 5 topics must have a reading.

High: more sources than usual carry notable news on a topic. Low: fewer sources than usual. The chart through the day shows each topic's News Heat minute by minute. Colours mark the cuts at 25, 75, 90 and 97.

Negative and positive news per topic

The same count as News Heat, on sources with at least one notable negative (or positive) story on the topic. The balance in each topic card is News Sentiment for that topic.

Topics with unusually negative or positive news

The number of topics whose negative (or positive) news reading is 90 or more, a level each topic reaches on about one comparable day in ten. Usual is the median of that number on the comparable days at the same time. The eight topics with negative and positive news count, so the number is out of 8. US politics (conflict, resolution) and Regulation (tightening, loosening) keep their own readings and flags but are not counted here.

Breadth

Only the notable stories of the ten topics count; stories with no topic and economic-data lines stay out. Per source: the mean grade of its notable stories so far today, ranked against the same source at the same time on its comparable days. A source needs at least 20 comparable days with stories. It counts as more serious than usual when its rank is 75 or more.

Breadth is the share of such sources among those read (at least 20), ranked against the same share on comparable days. A source counts once, however many stories it posts. Breadth has no value on 83 days at the start of the history (1 Aug 2025 to 20 Dec 2025): a source needs at least 20 comparable days with stories, and the day's share of sources needs at least 20 comparable days with a share. The first day with a value is 29 Sep 2025; on Saturdays and Sundays, which have fewer comparable days, values start later (last day without one: 20 Dec 2025).

Per topic, the same is computed from each source's notable stories on that topic: a source needs 15 comparable days with stories on the topic, and the topic needs 8 such sources; its share is then ranked against at least 20 comparable days with a share, and with fewer the table says so. Per-topic Breadth is computed at every minute, like the whole-day Breadth, and the page shows it at the page's minute.

High: many sources report more serious stories than they usually do. Low: few do.

News Sentiment

A notable story counts +1 when positive and -1 when negative; stories with no direction stay out. Per source: the mean of these values over its stories so far today, pulled toward the source's mean over the past year when it has few stories. That value is compared with the source's comparable days of the past year (standard score).

The reading averages the sources' scores (at least 2 sources) and ranks the average against the same average on comparable days of the past year at the same time. The day's reading uses eight topics: all except US politics and Regulation, whose sides describe a state.

High: more positive than usual. Low: more negative than usual. Bands: below 10 much more negative, 10 to 25 more negative, 25 to 75 a usual balance, 75 to 90 more positive, 90 or more much more positive.

News Velocity

Per source: its notable stories so far today, as a standard score against the same source at the same time on its comparable days, capped at 3 either way. The reading averages these scores over the sources active today and ranks the average against comparable days.

The figure "x usual" is the median over the active sources of each source's stories so far divided by its usual number by this time.

High: notable news arrives faster than usual. Low: slower.

News Velocity and its "x usual" figure measure posting speed: they count stories, so a source that posts more moves them.

Focus

Per source, the largest topic's share of its notable stories so far today, averaged over the active sources; the topic named is the one most often largest, and the usual value is the median on comparable days by this time.

High: one topic takes most of the day's notable news. Low: the news is spread across topics.

Consensus

Shown for Geopolitics at 14:00 and for US politics at the close. Per source: a value from the ranks of its notable stories on the topic so far today, where a story's rank places its grade among the topic's base-period stories of three reference sources; with few stories the value is pulled toward the source's usual level. Each source's value is ranked against its own comparable days.

The spread is the distance between the 25th and 75th percentile of these ranks across sources (at least 20 sources). Consensus is 100 minus the rank of today's spread against comparable days. It uses 111 of the 115 sources: it reads the source list of the engine it comes from, which predates the four website sources (BNO News, Interfax, InvestingLive, Trading Economics).

High: the sources rate the day alike. Low: some treat the day as exceptional while others report as usual.

Severe News Share

Per source, the share of its notable stories so far today graded 0.9 or more, averaged over the active sources, with its usual value (median on comparable days) and its rank against comparable days.

High: more of the day's notable news is at the top grade. Reports of attacks, strikes and fighting reach it most often.

Confluence tracker

A grid of the day's three readings: News Heat (the gauge), Breadth and News Sentiment for the whole day. A day with News Heat and Breadth both at 75 or more is Widely covered and serious, with negative or positive added when News Sentiment is 25 or below, or 75 or above. Other days: Busy, ordinary (News Heat 75 or more, Breadth below 50); Serious news, few sources (News Heat below 50, Breadth 75 or more); Quiet (both below 50); otherwise Mixed.

Flags

A flag goes up for a topic and side when, in the last 3 hours, more sources carried a notable story of that side than at the same time on every comparable day; at least 5 stories of that side from at least 3 sources; and the topic's News Heat is 75 or more.

A flag goes up after 15 minutes above that bar and ends 30 minutes after the topic falls below it. A topic never carries both flags at once. Flags shorter than 30 minutes are dropped. The overall flag (named 'Two or more topics' on the page) is up while two or more of the eight topics of News Sentiment are flagged on the same side.

From 1 Dec 2025 to 5 Nov 2026, some topic was flagged on 64% of days (216 of 340).

Cards and top story

The first card is always Geopolitics. The second is the day's top topic when that topic is not Geopolitics and the gauge reads 75 or more; otherwise it is the topic that reads 75 or more both against comparable days and against the last 4 weeks. Each topic card shows the sources carrying the topic today and usually, the negative and positive counts, the balance and the top story.

The top story is the group of matching stories (see Stories) carried by the most distinct sources so far today, at least 3; ties go to the group with more headlines, then to the earlier first report. The card shows the group's most typical headline: the one sharing the most words with the others, among those that state something (no question, no "could", "might" or "may"). With no such group the card shows the largest group of 2 sources, then the topic's earliest notable story.

Data releases

The card lists today's economic-data lines from the sources in the readings that state a figure. A release worded by two sources within 5 minutes counts once. It shows how many release lines arrived today and the latest three: US releases first, then others. Actual, forecast and previous come from the line itself; an up mark means the actual is above the forecast and a down mark below it.

6. Comparable days and the clock

The day is the calendar day in New York time, from 00:00 to 24:00. Every reading compares today up to the current time with the same hours on comparable days: a story counts when it was posted before that time of day.

Comparable days are the same kind of day in the previous 26 weeks (182 days): weekdays are compared with weekdays, Saturdays and Sundays with Saturdays and Sundays. News Sentiment uses the past year (365 days). A reading needs at least 8 comparable days; Breadth needs 20 comparable days per source.

US market holidays on a weekday follow one rule for every reading. A holiday is read twice, once against the working days of the window and once against its Saturdays and Sundays, each time with that set's usual levels, and the two results are averaged. Holidays are never comparable days for other days. Sources that post only on weekdays are left out on Saturdays and Sundays only, so they count on a weekday holiday.

Holidays in the record: 1 Sep 2025, 27 Nov 2025, 25 Dec 2025, 1 Jan 2026, 19 Jan 2026, 16 Feb 2026, 3 Apr 2026, 25 May 2026, 19 Jun 2026, 3 Jul 2026, 7 Sep 2026.

7. Updates, revisions and versions

  • Every 5 minutes: new posts are collected, scored, grouped into stories and labelled, and the page is rebuilt.
  • After each 5-minute run: every reading is computed for each minute since the previous run. The charts show one value per minute, and News Velocity, Focus, Severe News Share, Consensus and Breadth are shown at the page's minute.
  • Every night after New York midnight: the finished day is merged, the finished day and the day before are scored again with all their posts, the full history is rebuilt, the finished day's page is archived, and the historical data page and download files are written. Readings of the last two days can therefore change by a small amount when late posts arrive.
  • The grade rules and the label model are fixed between versions. A new label model replaces the current one only when it agrees better with the author's blind judgements. It then relabels the whole history, the readings are recomputed, and the series is released as a new version. Earlier versions stay downloadable.
  • Version 1 runs labeller version 6.

8. What the page does not do

  • News Pulse makes no market predictions. Market prices are never an input: every reading comes from headlines.
  • One account posting more of the same does not move News Heat, Breadth, Focus or Severe News Share: News Heat counts a source once per topic, Breadth uses each source's mean grade, Focus and Severe News Share average per-source shares, and every source is compared with its own past.
  • Each source counts once in News Sentiment and Consensus. A source that posts more stories gets a firmer estimate of its own tone, so its weight grows: when one account posted every story twice in a test, News Sentiment changed by at most 2 points (last 3 hours 8, a single topic 12) and Consensus on Geopolitics by at most 6. These figures are at the close. Within the day the same test moved single 5-minute values more: News Sentiment by up to 40 points (last 3 hours 12, a single topic 47) and Consensus on Geopolitics by up to 38.
  • News Velocity measures how fast stories arrive, so a source that posts more moves it.
  • Counts of headlines in the strip are context. They are not readings.

9. Data download and how to cite

The Historical data page offers the daily readings since 1 Aug 2025 at the close of each day (24:00 New York time): news_pulse_daily.csv (one row per day), news_pulse_topics_daily.csv (one row per day and topic) and a README, also as one zip file. The minute readings (every reading at every minute since 1 Aug 2025, all topics and by topic, one file per month, with README_minute.txt) are a second zip file. Headlines and per-source data are not included. A finished day's minute rows are recomputed in the night from the whole day (for example, whether a source posted enough that day to count), so they can differ from what the page showed live at that minute: on 5 Nov 2026 before 12:40, News Velocity differed on 754 of 760 minutes (by up to 24 points) and Consensus on Geopolitics on 396 (up to 38); README_minute.txt gives the other readings.
