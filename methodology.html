What News Pulse reads

The headlines of about 110 English news sources on Bluesky and Telegram, collected minute by minute. Each headline is given a topic (one of ten), a grade from 0 to 1 by what it reports (an attack or a strike grades higher than a threat, a statement or a forecast) and, where the topic has one, a direction. Headlines graded 0.5 or more are notable. Repeats of the same story by a source, and follow-ups within 72 hours, are counted once.

Comparable days

Every 0–100 reading compares today, up to the current time, with the same hours on comparable days: the same kind of day (weekday, or Saturday and Sunday) in the past 26 weeks. 80 means higher than on 80 of 100 of those days; 50 is a typical day.

News Heat

How widely the news is covered: the number of sources carrying notable news on each topic, against that topic's usual number. The gauge shows the day's top topic, the one furthest above its usual count. Bands: Cool 0–25, Normal 25–75, Warm 75–90, Hot 90–97, Very hot 97–100.

Breadth

How serious the news is: the share of sources whose notable stories today are more serious than usual for that source. Each source counts once, however many stories it posts.

News Sentiment

The balance of positive and negative stories, each source against its own past. 50 is a usual balance; below 50 the news is more negative than usual. For US politics the direction is conflict or resolution and for Regulation tightening or loosening; these are shown in neutral colours.

News Velocity, Consensus, Shock Share, Focus

News Velocity: how fast notable news arrives. Consensus: how alike the sources rate Geopolitics (at 14:00) and US politics (at the close). Shock Share: the share of notable stories at the top grade, mostly attacks, strikes and fighting. Focus: the largest topic's share of the day's notable stories against its usual share.

Flags and the Confluence tracker

A negative-news (positive-news) flag is raised when more sources reported negative (positive) news on a topic in the last 3 hours than at that time on any comparable day. The Confluence tracker marks a major news day when News Heat and Breadth are both 75 or more, and calls it negative or positive when News Sentiment is 25 or below or 75 or above.

Labels

Topics and directions come from a language model trained on headlines labelled by a larger model and checked against the author's own blind judgements. The model version is fixed between scheduled revisions; a revision relabels the whole history and is recorded with its date.
