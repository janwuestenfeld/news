News Pulse condenses what 115 English news sources around the globe report into a handful of readings, minute by minute: how hot the news is, how serious it is and which way it leans. A text model trained for this page reads every headline and gives it a topic, a grade for how serious it is and a direction. Every reading exists for each of the ten topics, from Geopolitics and US politics to central banks and trade, and for all news together:

  • News Heat: how widely the news is covered
  • Breadth: how many sources report more serious news than they usually do
  • News Sentiment: whether it is positive or negative
  • News Velocity, Consensus, Focus and Severe News Share: how fast the news is coming in, whether sources agree, whether one topic dominates and how much of the news is at the most serious level

Each reading compares today with the same hours on comparable days. A topic gets a red flag when more sources have reported negative news on it in the last three hours than at that time on any comparable day, and a green flag for positive news. For each topic, a card shows the day's largest story and how many sources carried it. The Confluence tracker shows News Heat, Breadth and News Sentiment day by day and marks the days when the news is both widely covered and serious.

How to read the numbers: every 0–100 number compares today with comparable days (the same kind of day in the past 26 weeks; for News Sentiment, in the past year). 80 means higher than on 80 of 100 of them; 50 is a typical day.
